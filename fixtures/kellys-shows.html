SUPER CASSETTE (SF) W TBA

Before their viral queer-joy anthem “Be Gay, Do Drugs, Hail Satan” launched them into the public eye, or their indie bop “Sober” made waves on Latin American YouTube, Oakland’s Super Cassette began as the modest bedroom project of frontperson Max Gerlock (they/them). After teaming up with bassist Devin Hollister and, later, drummer Ramon Esquivel (who joined after taking a chance on a flyer), the trio evolved into a tightly-wound power-pop band known for inventive chord progressions, bittersweet lyrics, and a high-energy, uplifting live show featuring the impressive musicianship of each member. Super Cassette has toured extensively across the U.S. (most recently with Moon Walker) and is in the process of recording their second album, which promises to mix the trio's dynamic live performances with Max’s meticulously-layered production.

Monday · 8:30 PM

THE NATIONAL FOREST

The National Forest is the solo indie folk and Americana project of Logan Bowden, a singer-songwriter, producer, and multi-instrumentalist originally from Huntsville, Alabama. With no real folk scene in his hometown to lean on, he learned every piece of DIY audio engineering himself, from tracking to mixing. In late 2023, he started The National Forest as a way to quiet his inner critic: write, record, and release a song every single day. That stretch of relentless output produced his first collections, which have a raw, warm, and intimate acoustic feel. Built on layered acoustic guitar, banjo, introspective lyrics, and rich vocal harmonies, the music began with a lo-fi bedroom closeness and has since grown into something more cinematic and rooted in nature. Fans of Gregory Alan Isakov, Iron & Wine, Caamp, and Andy Shauf will feel at home here. Releasing music through Tiny House Records and working entirely DIY, The National Forest has earned millions of streams worldwide. The self-titled The National Forest (2024) and Letters (2025) were recorded in a self-built studio in the Pacific Northwest, and they explore growing up, changing seasons, and a “cowboy folk” sound.
